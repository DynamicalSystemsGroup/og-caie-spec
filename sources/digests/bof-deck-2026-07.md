# Building Scientific Approaches to Generative AI (BoF deck, SciPy 2026) — digest

Source: `src:bof-deck-2026-07`, Zargham and Hollek. Slide 5, "Helpful
Definitions" (the authors' own wording; canonical source named on the slide:
Popper 1959):

- "A hypothesis is a conjectural claim that can be subjected to empirical tests and potentially falsified. Its empirical content lies partly in what it rules out: the more possible outcomes it excludes, the greater the risk it takes."
- "An (auxiliary) assumption is a background claim held fixed in deriving and testing a prediction. Because predictions normally follow from a hypothesis together with auxiliary assumptions and initial conditions, a failed prediction does not by logic alone identify which component is false."
- "A prediction is an observable consequence that should occur if the hypothesis and relevant auxiliary assumptions are true. An observation that should not occur under those conditions is a potential falsifier."
- "Evidence is an accepted result of observation or experiment."
- "Falsifiability is the property of excluding at least one possible empirical outcome."
- "Context Matters!"
