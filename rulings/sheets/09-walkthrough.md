## The record's fan-out: analysis (walkthrough B9)

Today the recorder has one output, the record, on six wires: to the authorized representative, the domain expert, the evaluation operator, the test driver, the conformance checker and the report assembler (six `recordIn` ports, six seams; the views bundle them into one edge per reader). What each reader actually does with it: the driver derives probes from the DSO release and the requirement set; the checker runs the shapes; the assembler recomputes coverage into the report; the expert determines and attests on evidence and approves the report; the operator writes the plan and the recommendation from the record; the representative delivers the report. Three options:

| Option | Model | What it says | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) keep | 6 wires, 6 ports | every reader has a wire | six seams and ports that carry nothing but "may read"; the M-shapes prove nothing about them beyond presence |
| (b) machines by wire, people by view | the record wires only to the three machines (driver, checker, assembler); the people read the record through their role's view of it, which the report step and the views registry make explicit; the human ports and their three seams go | the record feeds forward through the machines into the report; humans review and sign off on what the machines produce and on the evidence the record holds, reached by a view tailored to the role | the process DAG is unchanged (steps consume items by flow, not by port); M3-RecordHub unchanged; three seams and three ports fewer; the executor unchanged |
| (c) one publication | one wire to an abstract "record reader" part that the six specialize | one wire drawn | a new abstraction for a drawing convenience; nothing checkable gained |

Recommended: (b), with the distribution of the report to the sponsor (the delivery) and, in prose, potentially to the test item provider (monitoring), as today.
